What First-Time Buyers Need to Know

Purchasing your first home involves more than just the sticker price. Here are the key financial factors to understand:

  • Down payment — while 20% down avoids private mortgage insurance (PMI), many first-time buyers put down 5–10%. FHA loans allow as little as 3.5%. A lower down payment increases your monthly costs but gets you into a home sooner.
  • PMI costs — if your down payment is below 20%, expect to pay PMI of roughly 0.5–1.0% of the loan amount annually. On a $247,500 loan, that is about $100–$200 per month on top of your mortgage payment.
  • Closing costs — budget 2–5% of the home price for closing costs (appraisal, title insurance, origination fees, taxes). On a $275,000 home, that is $5,500–$13,750.
  • Monthly budget rule — most financial advisors recommend keeping total housing costs (including taxes and insurance) below 28% of your gross monthly income.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do I need to save before buying my first home?

At minimum, you need your down payment plus closing costs plus an emergency reserve (3–6 months of expenses). For a $275,000 home with 10% down, that means roughly $27,500 (down) + $8,000–$14,000 (closing) + emergency fund. Many first-time buyer programs offer down payment assistance — check your state's housing agency for options.

What credit score do I need for a mortgage?

Conventional loans typically require a minimum score of 620, though better rates are available at 740+. FHA loans accept scores as low as 580 with 3.5% down (or 500 with 10% down). Your credit score significantly impacts your interest rate — even a 0.5% difference changes your monthly payment by $80–$100 on a typical first home.

Should I choose a 15-year or 30-year mortgage?

Most first-time buyers choose a 30-year mortgage for the lower monthly payment. This makes sense when cash flow is tight. A 15-year mortgage saves substantial interest but requires about 40–50% higher monthly payments. Start with 30 years and consider refinancing to 15 as your income grows.
