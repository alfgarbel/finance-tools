The $350K Mortgage: Above-Median Borrowing

A $350,000 mortgage sits above the national median loan amount, making it typical for suburban homes in mid-to-high cost areas, newer construction, and larger properties in desirable markets. At a 6.5% fixed rate over 30 years, the monthly principal and interest payment is approximately $2,212. Over the full term, total interest costs reach roughly $446,320, meaning you would pay about $796,320 in total for your $350,000 loan.

Using the 28% housing-cost guideline, a gross annual income of approximately $95,000 is the minimum to comfortably cover the principal and interest alone. When you add property taxes, homeowner's insurance, and potential HOA fees, the recommended income rises to $115,000 or more depending on your local tax rates.

Managing a $350K Mortgage Responsibly

At $350,000, your mortgage is a substantial long-term commitment. Smart management makes the difference between financial stress and building lasting wealth through homeownership.

  • Keep housing costs at or below 28% of gross income — this means your total monthly housing payment (principal, interest, taxes, and insurance) should stay within a comfortable range for your income level.
  • Build an emergency fund of three to six months' expenses — homeownership brings unexpected repair costs. Having liquid savings separate from your down payment protects you from financial disruption.
  • Consider one extra payment per year — on a 30-year $350K mortgage, making one additional payment annually can save over $100,000 in total interest and shorten your loan by approximately five to seven years.
  • Compare rates aggressively before closing — on a $350K loan, a 0.25% rate difference saves roughly $17,500 over 30 years. Getting quotes from multiple lenders is one of the most valuable steps you can take.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the monthly payment on a $350K mortgage?

At a 6.5% fixed interest rate over 30 years, the monthly principal and interest payment on a $350,000 mortgage is approximately $2,212. With a 15-year term at the same rate, the monthly payment rises to about $3,049, but you save roughly $247,500 in total interest. Your actual payment will also include property taxes and insurance, which vary by location.

What income do you need for a $350K mortgage?

Using the 28% housing-cost guideline, you need roughly $95,000 to $115,000 per year in gross income to comfortably afford a $350,000 mortgage. Property taxes, homeowner's insurance, and HOA fees increase the recommended income further. Lenders will also review your total debt-to-income ratio, which should stay below 43%.

Is a 15-year mortgage worth it at $350K?

If you can comfortably afford the higher monthly payment — approximately $837 more per month compared to a 30-year term at 6.5% — a 15-year mortgage on a $350,000 loan saves roughly $247,500 in total interest and builds equity much faster. The key is ensuring the higher payment fits your budget without straining your savings or emergency fund.

What to Look For When Comparing Mortgage Rates

Even a 0.25% difference in your mortgage rate changes your total interest paid by tens of thousands of dollars over a 30-year term. When evaluating lenders, consider these factors alongside the headline rate:

  • APR vs. interest rate — the APR includes origination fees and gives a more accurate total-cost comparison across lenders
  • Points — paying discount points upfront lowers your rate but extends the break-even period; worthwhile only if you stay in the home long enough
  • Fixed vs. adjustable — ARMs start lower but carry rate-reset risk after the initial period; fixed rates offer long-term payment certainty
  • Lender type — banks, credit unions, and online lenders each offer different rate structures, underwriting timelines, and service models
  • Rate lock period — confirm how long the quoted rate is guaranteed during the underwriting process before you are committed
