Why Choose a 15-Year Mortgage?

A 15-year fixed-rate mortgage is one of the most efficient ways to build home equity and minimize interest costs. Here is what makes it compelling:

  • Massive interest savings — you pay interest for half as many years, and 15-year mortgages typically carry lower interest rates (often 0.5–0.75% lower than 30-year rates). The combined effect can save you 50–60% in total interest.
  • Faster equity buildup — because each payment retires more principal, you own your home outright in 15 years. This provides financial security and flexibility in your peak earning years.
  • Lower rates available — lenders view 15-year mortgages as lower risk, so they typically offer more favorable interest rates compared to 30-year loans.

The trade-off is a higher monthly payment — typically 40–50% more than a 30-year mortgage for the same loan amount. Make sure the higher payment fits comfortably within your budget before committing.

Is a 15-Year Mortgage Right for You?

A 15-year mortgage works best when:

  • Your monthly income comfortably covers the higher payment with room to spare for emergencies and other savings goals.
  • You plan to stay in the home for at least 5–10 years, giving you time to benefit from accelerated equity growth.
  • You are refinancing an existing 30-year mortgage and have increased your income since the original purchase.
  • You are nearing retirement and want to eliminate mortgage debt before you stop working.

If the higher monthly payment stretches your budget too thin, a 30-year mortgage with occasional extra principal payments can be a flexible compromise. Use the main mortgage calculator to compare both options.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much more is a 15-year payment compared to a 30-year?

Typically 40–50% higher. For a $280,000 loan, a 15-year mortgage at 5.75% costs about $2,330/month versus roughly $1,770/month for a 30-year at 6.50%. The extra $560/month buys you freedom from mortgage debt 15 years sooner and saves over $200,000 in interest.

Are 15-year mortgage rates lower than 30-year rates?

Yes. Lenders typically offer rates 0.5–0.75 percentage points lower on 15-year mortgages because the shorter term reduces the lender's risk exposure. This rate difference further amplifies the interest savings.

Can I refinance from a 30-year to a 15-year mortgage?

Absolutely. Refinancing to a 15-year term is a popular strategy when interest rates drop or your income increases. You will need to qualify under the lender's current guidelines and pay closing costs, but the long-term savings often justify the switch — especially if you have already paid several years on your 30-year loan.
