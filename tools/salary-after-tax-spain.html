How Salary After Tax Is Estimated in Spain

In Spain, employees pay two main types of deductions from their gross salary:

  1. Social Security contributions — the employee share is approximately 6.35% of gross salary, capped at the maximum contribution base (currently around €56,600/year). This funds public pensions, unemployment, and healthcare.
  2. Personal Income Tax (IRPF) — a progressive tax applied to your taxable income (gross minus social security). Spain uses a split system where roughly half is set by the state and half by the autonomous community, but the combined brackets follow a progressive structure.

This calculator uses a simplified progressive model that approximates the combined state + regional IRPF rates. A small personal allowance is applied depending on your tax profile (single or married).

Gross Salary vs. Net Salary

Gross salary is the total amount your employer pays before any deductions. It includes your base salary plus any bonuses or benefits that are subject to tax.

Net salary (take-home pay) is what you actually receive after social security contributions and income tax have been deducted. In Spain, the difference between gross and net can range from about 15% for low salaries to over 40% for very high earners.

In Spain it is common to receive 14 payments per year instead of 12. The annual gross is the same, but it is divided into 14 installments — the two extra payments (known as pagas extra) are traditionally paid in June and December.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are there 14 payments in Spain?

Spanish labor law traditionally provides for two extra salary payments (pagas extraordinarias) — one in summer (typically June) and one at Christmas. The total annual gross remains the same; it is simply divided into 14 installments instead of 12. Some companies allow employees to choose between 12 or 14 payments.

How accurate is this calculator?

This calculator provides a reasonable approximation for general planning. It uses simplified IRPF brackets and a flat social security rate. Actual take-home pay may differ depending on your autonomous community's regional tax rates, specific deductions (children, disability, mortgage), employment type, and other factors.

Does this include employer social security costs?

No. This calculator only shows deductions from the employee's perspective. Employers in Spain pay an additional ~30% on top of gross salary for their share of social security, but that does not affect your take-home pay.
